## Your working time in Hinata

This instance records working time. This notice explains what is stored about you, why, who can see it and which rights you have. The panel **Who sees my time data?** directly below it shows the rules that are switched on right now. If it and this text ever seem to disagree, the panel is right.

### Why your time is recorded

- to document working time where the law requires it (§ 16 (2) ArbZG, § 17 MiLoG),
- to account for time spent on projects and issues,
- where switched on: to hand timesheets in for approval, and for the reports and billing your organisation uses.

Your time is never used for automated decisions about you. Hints and reminders are hints, nothing more.

### What is stored

- your time entries: day, duration, optional start and end, project, issue, activity, description, tags, whether it is billable, and when it was created or changed and by whom,
- a running timer while it runs, and your timer settings,
- timesheets you hand in, and the decisions and notes on them,
- correction requests and their answers,
- when you confirmed this notice.

### Who receives it

You always see your own entries. Administrators of this instance can see every entry. Whether project leads see your individual entries, whether timesheets are approved and which reports exist depends on the rules your organisation has switched on. The panel lists them.

### How long it is kept

Entries are project records and are kept until the retention period set by your organisation ends; the panel shows it. If your account is deleted, your entries stay under a pseudonym; the account, a running timer and timesheets that were not yet approved are deleted.

### Your rights

- **Access and a copy** (Art. 15, 20 GDPR): Settings → Time tracking exports your entries as CSV; the account export contains all your time data.
- **Rectification** (Art. 16): edit your own entries. Where a day is locked, request a correction. Your reason is recorded and goes to the person who can open the day.
- **Erasure and restriction** (Art. 17, 18), **objection** (Art. 21): contact your organisation's privacy contact.
- **Complaint** to a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77).

The organisation operating this instance is responsible for the processing. Its privacy policy names the contact for questions.
